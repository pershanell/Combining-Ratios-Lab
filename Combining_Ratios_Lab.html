Shells and the Periodic Table are linked. Number of occupied shells = period. Number of outer-shell electrons = group (Groups I–VII). A full outer shell = Group VIII/0.

Valency is the number of electrons an atom must lose, gain or share to get a full outer shell. Atoms take the easier route — the one that moves fewer electrons.

Ionic compounds: metal atoms give electrons to non-metal atoms. Every electron given away must be taken by someone, so the total positive charge must equal the total negative charge. That fixes the ratio of ions.

Covalent molecules: non-metal atoms share pairs of electrons. Each atom has a fixed number of "bonding hands" (its valency). A molecule is finished when every hand is holding another hand — so the ratio of atoms is fixed.
Each line = one shared pair of electrons (one electron from each atom). Red stubs = empty hands still needing a partner.

Key formulae
